# Level 1 evaluation against GHS-POP: results, reading guide, limitations

Bundles: `eval/india-full/india-l1-pop-YYYY.eval.json` (one machine-readable bundle per year),
`india-l1-pop-YYYY.run.json` (production report), `policy_2011_comparison.json`, `manifest.json`,
`figures/` (national 10 km residual map every year; 100 m zooms for Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Bengaluru and
rural Bihar in 2001, 2006, 2011). `eval/synthetic/` holds the SYNTHETIC software-test run and is **not**
science. Method: `../METHOD.md` §8. Tables below are generated by `scripts/handoff_tables.py` from these JSON
files (all 11 years are in `../HANDOFF_REPORT.md`).

## What was compared

* **Model**: Level 1 COG `india-l1-pop-YYYY` (people per ~100 m cell). **Baseline**: the unmodified WorldPop
  count for the same year (so the effect of the redistribution is isolated).
* **Reference**: GHS-POP R2023A 3 arc-second, on the identical lattice (no regridding). Exact epoch for 2005
  and 2010. For the other nine years it is a *linear interpolation of the two bracketing GHS-POP epochs*
  (`reference_temporal = interpolated`), and for 2011 it interpolates 2010–2015.
* **Common support and unit**: people per support unit, over cells where Level 1 is valid: native 100 m
  cells, 1 km and 10 km blocks, and ADM1 (36) / ADM2 (735) zones. GHS-POP population outside the Level 1
  valid mask is reported separately as coverage, not silently dropped.

| 100 m, systematic 1/289 sample | Spearman | 0.628 | 0.386 |
| 100 m | MAE (people/cell) | 2.90 | 4.25 |
| 100 m | RMSE (people/cell) | 18.8 | 18.3 |

Across 2001–2011 the 100 m Pearson is 0.50–0.56, the Spearman 0.59–0.63, the MAE 2.7–2.95, and the 1 km
Pearson 0.73–0.76. District-level Pearson is 0.999 in every year.

## How to read it (honest reading)

1. **Level 1 is closer to GHS-POP per cell on average (MAE about 30–32 % lower in every year) and in rank order (Spearman about
   0.6 vs 0.3–0.4), but its Pearson at 100 m is slightly lower (about 0.03) and RMSE slightly higher than
   raw WorldPop.** The Pearson/RMSE are dominated by a few thousand extreme urban cells; the MAE and
   Spearman describe the bulk. These are reported as measured. No threshold was imposed.
2. **1 km, 10 km and district results are identical to WorldPop's by construction** (population is conserved
   inside each 10 × 10-cell parent). They therefore measure WorldPop vs GHS-POP, not the redistribution. The
   redistribution is visible only at 100 m and at zone boundaries that cut through parents.
3. **GHS-POP is not ground truth and not independent.** It is a modelled product that is itself built on
   GHSL built-up surface, so agreement in the spatial pattern at 100 m partly reflects shared inputs. It also
   disaggregates census totals, whereas WorldPop is not UN-adjusted here: Level 1 totals are 3–4 % below
   GHS-POP's over the same cells (`rel_bias_total`, −0.038 in 2001 → −0.029 in 2011). That difference is a
   property of the two sources, not an error introduced by the redistribution (conservation is exact).
4. **Coverage.** 1.1 % of GHS-POP's India population (12–14 million people per year) lies outside the Level 1
   valid mask, in cells where WorldPop-India is NoData. The bundles do not attribute this by location; it is probably the
   border and disputed-territory footprint difference (parts of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh), but that was not
   verified cell by cell. About 1.1–1.2 million Level 1 people lie outside every geoBoundaries ADM1 polygon (coast and
   boundary-simplification effects). Both numbers are in each bundle under `coverage`.
5. **2011 policy.** The three post-2010 options are compared in `policy_2011_comparison.json`.
   Against GHS-POP, option C (approved) is marginally best (Pearson 0.5613 vs 0.5570/0.5574; MAE 2.902 vs 2.921/2.921). The
   differences are small relative to the 100 m noise.
6. **The WorldPop 2010 file has a different footprint** (1,114,365 additional valid cells, holding 4,311
   people in total, 99.98 % of them zero) and one extra west column (31 cells, 0 people). Both are handled
   and reported (`registration_to_reference`, `outside_reference_grid`). 2010 therefore has 4,053,138 populated
   parents versus 4,017,858 in the other years.

## Conservation at administrative support (measured for 2011; other years are in their bundles)

Level 1 conserves population **exactly per parent (10 × 10 cells) and nationally** (relative error ~10⁻¹²).
State and district boundaries cut through parents, so people can move across a boundary inside one parent.
Measured against WorldPop's own zonal totals (`conservation_vs_worldpop` in each bundle, per-zone rows under
`zones`):

| Zones | n | median |Δ|/WorldPop | 95th pct | 99th pct | max | Σ|Δ| / national total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| States (ADM1) | 36 | 0.04 % | 1.6 % | 2.6 % | 2.7 % (Puducherry: fragmented enclaves) | 0.04 % |
| Districts (ADM2) | 735 | 0.11 % | 0.9 % | 2.0 % | 18 % (a 37 000-person unit; Jiribam and Mahe next) | 0.15 % |

The large relative differences are in very small or fragmented units (islands, enclaves, slivers), where a
single ~1 km parent is a large share of the unit. This is a consequence of choosing a 10 × 10-cell parent
(decision D-L1-02, approved). If exact district totals are required, the two-pass administrative-zone parent
mode (`core.zone_sums` / `core.redistribute_zones`, tested) can be run with authoritative Census-2011 boundaries
from Agent 1. It was not run, because those boundaries were not available.

## Limitations

* The reference is interpolated for 9 of 11 years (exact only in 2005 and 2010).
* The 100 m Spearman uses a deterministic systematic sample (1 in 289 cells, n ≈ 1.4·10⁶), not all cells.
* ADM1/ADM2 zones come from geoBoundaries (2011/2021 vintages), not authoritative Census-2011 boundaries
  (requested from Agent 1). They are used for diagnostic aggregation only.
* The evaluation cannot validate against census counts. Census/town-level comparison belongs to Level 2
  (Agent 3).
