# Level 2 method for selected 2011 settlements

**Primary method approved by the user on 9 November 2026.** Allocate population only to classes that the approved analytical Bhuvan legend identifies as residential or settlement. Use the other classes for the coverage accounting and diagnostic comparisons. Verify the code-to-class crosswalk against the analytical delivery and record the rationale for each sensitivity weight. A category whose eligibility is ambiguous requires a decision if including it changes the primary allocation.

The analytical product must distinguish eligible settlement classes from known nonresidential land. Do not apply the hard mask to the aggregated web urban group. Where no applicable accuracy figure is available, record it as unknown. ## Support, grid and prior

- Join exact 2011 Census PCA totals and matching non-ward settlement polygons by full location code. Reject duplicates, missing rows, mismatched names or vintage, invalid polygons and any positive-area overlap beyond the geometry tolerance. Preserve holes and multipart boundaries.
- Project polygons to WGS 84 / NSIDC EASE-Grid 2.0 Global (EPSG:6933). Densify geographic edges at 0.001° before projection. The grid is north-up, anchored at x = y = 0 and spaced at exactly 23.5 m on each axis. Town windows snap outward to the common lattice. EPSG:6933 is equal-area, so the projected cell area is 552.25 m². Local ground lengths in the two directions can differ from 23.5 m.
- Each fine cell has a single owner across the selected set. Choose the town with the largest positive polygon intersection. Round area to 10⁻⁷ m² for tie-breaking and prefer the lower Census ID. Use only the area of the winning polygon inside the cell. Report edge area lost to another town. The shared lattice keeps valid pixels from overlapping, and the final sum still matches each Census control.
- Give each target cell one class using the vector at its centre, or nearest-neighbour for a categorical raster. Conflicting vector classes with positive-area overlap at a sampled centre fail. Values from unknown classes fail. Same-class overlaps are harmless. If the rasterizer draws both sides of a shared edge at the same centre, the higher normalized class code takes precedence. Raster NoData and vector gaps remain unclassified.
- Use the accepted 2011 Level 1 count product as the WorldPop-derived spatial prior. No further multiplication by WorldPop is needed. Divide each native Level 1 count by the area of its cell on the equal-area grid to obtain a piecewise-constant density. EPSG:4326 meridians and parallels remain axis-aligned after projection to EPSG:6933. Compute exact rectangle intersections on this grid, with polygon intersection at fractional edges. This is an area-based transfer of counts. The fine grid carries model estimates and inherits the spatial limitations of Census, Level 1 and LULC.

## Census constraint and fallback

For owned fine cell i, let Aᵢ be its intersection area with the town, and let Qᵢ be the sum of Level 1 density times the valid native Level 1 area overlapping that owned polygon fragment. The primary weight is Qᵢ × Eᵢ, where Eᵢ is 1 for an approved settlement or residential class and 0 elsewhere. Normalize those weights to the 2011 Census total T for the unit:

`Pᵢ = T × (Qᵢ × Eᵢ) / Σⱼ (Qⱼ × Eⱼ)`

When all primary weights are zero and there is eligible area, replace Qᵢ with Aᵢ. This fallback allocates uniformly by eligible area. A positive Census count with zero eligible area fails. Known excluded classes have a valid count of zero. Unclassified cells, holes, the exterior and cells owned by another town are NoData. A known zero-population unit can produce valid zeros and still requires comparable source support.

Write lossless float32 COGs with NoData −99999. Sum the stored values in float64 and fail outside max(0.001 person, 10⁻⁶ × T). Population counts remain fractional to preserve proportional allocation. Internal overviews are for display. The run records source and common support, including source gaps. The supplied real template proposes a 95% minimum for each source over the Census polygon. Set and record these readiness cutoffs before modeling. The cutoffs measure data availability and are independent of residual size.

## Evaluation and uncertainty

1. Compare the stored Level 2 sum with PCA to validate the allocation constraint. Separately intersect the entire Census polygon with native valid Level 1 cells and report the Level 1 estimate, signed and relative difference versus PCA, and valid area coverage. Partial-coverage Level 1 totals describe observed support and may understate whole-town totals.
2. For the comparison on common support, intersect every classified and owned Level 2 fragment with each valid native Level 1 cell. For Level 1 multiply the native density by the intersection area. For Level 2 split the stored count in proportion to that fragment’s intersected area, assuming a uniform distribution within the fragment. Aggregate the two independently to native cells and report bias, MAE, RMSE, total difference, signed relative total difference, sum of absolute residuals divided by the Level 1 total, and area and population excluded from comparison. Relative metrics are undefined if the reference total is zero. The three comparison raster bands contain Level 1, Level 2 and Level 2 minus Level 1 on identical support.
3. Recompute with a configured soft class weight in [0, 1] for each legend label. A soft weight of zero excludes that class. Low positive weights can represent plausible dwelling in mixed or agricultural classes where justified by the legend. Record the normalized population per class, alternative feasibility and half the cellwise L1 distance between both estimates (people moved, with share of the Census total). This measures sensitivity to the class policy. Differences may also reflect census-boundary error, the 2011 versus 2011–12 timing, mixed classes and Level 1 uncertainty. The two levels share a prior, and Census totals are imposed. Neither comparison estimates fine-scale predictive accuracy.
